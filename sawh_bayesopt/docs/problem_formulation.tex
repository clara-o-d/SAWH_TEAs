\section*{Scope}

This document states the optimization problem that \code{sawh\_bayesopt} solves, how the
surrogate it relies on is built and selected, how the optimizer is tuned and validated, and
the approximations each step makes. The physics behind the day map $F$ is in
\code{solar\_lumped/docs/governing\_eq.tex}. Code references are to
\code{sawh\_bayesopt/src/sawh\_bayesopt/} unless noted otherwise. Algorithm numbers refer to
Kochenderfer and Wheeler, \emph{Algorithms for Optimization}, 2nd edition.

The device is the PAM--LiCl hydrogel solar SAWH panel. It runs one cycle per day: it
absorbs at night with the panel open, then it is sealed and desorbs while the sun is up. The
problem is posed once per map cell (one Open-Meteo site), over one weather year (2024,
$D = 366$ days).

\paragraph{Design.} Hydrogel thickness is the only design variable
(\code{simulate.THICKNESS\_BOUNDS\_M}, from the \code{parameters.xlsx} sweep column):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X} = [\SI{1}{\milli\metre}, \SI{10}{\milli\metre}].
\end{equation}
The other two former design variables are fixed rather than optimized. The previous
three-variable campaign put salt loading at its upper bound almost everywhere (median 8.0),
so it is held there, $SL = 8$. Its vapor-gap optima scattered over the whole
\SIrange{7}{60}{\milli\metre} range, which means LCOW is nearly flat in the gap. The gap is
therefore tied to the thickness,
\begin{equation}
L_g = H_0 + \SI{7}{\milli\metre},
\end{equation}
because the gel is cast at $H_0$ and must fit inside the gap: at the \SI{7}{\milli\metre}
minimum gap, any $H_0$ above about \SI{6}{\milli\metre} would not. A fixed headroom keeps
the whole thickness box buildable, while the swelling ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:full}) still
penalizes over-swelling.

\paragraph{Control grid} (\code{simulate}, \code{optimize.candidates}). The offsets move
the seal and open times relative to GHI sunrise and sunset:
\begin{equation}
\delta_s, \delta_o \in \mathcal{S} = \{-4, -3.75, \dots, 4\}\,\si{\hour},
\qquad |\mathcal{S}|^2 = 1089.
\end{equation}
Negative $\delta_s$ seals before dawn; positive $\delta_o$ stays shut past dusk. Both edges
are clamped so that each phase keeps at least one sample
(\code{solar\_lumped.weather.\_shifted\_desorption\_mask}). The \SI{0.25}{\hour} step equals
the weather time step, so a finer offset would not change the physics.

The sealed window is split into three chronological segments of equal duration, each held
at its own tilt. Daily tilt is the case $\theta^{(1)} = \theta^{(2)} = \theta^{(3)}$. The
tilt grids are
\begin{equation}
\Theta_5 = \{0, 5, \dots, 60\}\si{\degree} \;(13 \text{ levels}),
\qquad
\Theta_{15} = \{0, 15, \dots, 60\}\si{\degree} \;(5 \text{ levels}).
\end{equation}
Daily and seasonal tilt search $\Theta_5$; three-per-day tilt searches $\Theta_{15}$ in each
segment ($5^3 = 125$ combinations, against $13^3 = 2197$ at \SI{5}{\degree}).

\paragraph{Held fixed} (not decision variables): LiCl salt at $SL = 8$; Case 2 optics and IR
emissivities; insulation gap \SI{5}{\milli\metre}; condenser fin area ratio $A_r = 7.1$;
$h_{amb} = \SI{10}{\watt\per\square\metre\per\kelvin}$; an isosteric (temperature- and
concentration-dependent) heat of desorption; all financial parameters
(\code{LCOEconomicParams}). Each cell's ambient pressure comes from its elevation. All of
these are \code{SystemConfig}'s defaults (\code{simulate.system\_config}).

\section{The day map and the year}

One simulated day is a map, from the JAX physics (\code{solar\_lumped.jax\_daily\_cycle}):
\begin{equation}
\bigl(m_d,\; s_{d+1},\; \kappa_d\bigr) = F\bigl(x, u_d, s_d;\, w_{c,d}\bigr),
\label{eq:daymap}
\end{equation}
where $m_d$ is the water collected that day (\si{\kilogram\per\square\metre}) and
$\kappa_d \in \{0,1\}$ flags a gel that reached the swelling ceiling (the gel--condenser
clearance) during the day.

Tilt enters the desorption phase twice at every time step: through the plane-of-array
transposition of GHI (Erbs decomposition, Liu--Jordan isotropic sky) and through the
Hollands $\cos\theta$ term in the vapor-gap convection. With a three-segment schedule, both
read the segment's tilt. The profile carries a per-step tilt
(\code{weather.PhaseProfile.tilt\_deg}), and the CPU and JAX backends both swap it into the
gap convection step by step (\code{solar\_lumped/tests/test\_cpu\_jax\_parity.py} holds them
to $5\times10^{-4}$ under such a schedule).

The gel carries its state from one day to the next, so the year is a chained trajectory.
Its start is the cyclic steady state of day 1,
\begin{equation}
s_1 = s^\ast \quad\text{with}\quad s^\ast = F_s\bigl(x, u_1, s^\ast;\, w_{c,1}\bigr),
\label{eq:cyclic}
\end{equation}
which the physics finds by Aitken $\Delta^2$ extrapolation. The annual figures of merit are
\begin{equation}
\bar m(x, u_{1:D}) = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D} m_d,
\qquad
\bar\kappa(x, u_{1:D}) = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D} \kappa_d .
\end{equation}

\section{Objective: levelized cost of water}

The cost per m$^2$ of panel is (\code{solar\_lumped.economics.lcow\_from\_daily\_yield}):
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{LCOW}(x, \bar m) =
\frac{\bigl(\mathrm{CRF}\cdot f_{TI} + f_{OM}\bigr)\,C_{sys} + C_{sorb}(x)}
{f_{util}\cdot 365\,\bar m/\rho_w}
\quad [\text{USD/m}^3],
\label{eq:lcow}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CRF} = \frac{i(1+i)^L}{(1+i)^L - 1},
\qquad
C_{sorb}(x) = \left[\frac{p_{salt}\,SL + p_{poly}}{1 + SL} + r_w\,p_{w}\right]
\frac{\rho_{dry}\,H_0}{L_{gel}} .
\label{eq:csorb}
\end{equation}
The symbols are: $C_{sys}$, the flat system bill of materials; $f_{TI}$, the
total-investment factor; $f_{OM}$, the maintenance fraction; $f_{util}$, utilization; $i$
and $L$, the discount rate and system life; and $L_{gel}$, the gel replacement interval.
$C_{sorb}$ is the annualized cost of re-casting the gel: the dry composite mass
$\rho_{dry}H_0$, priced at the salt/polymer blend plus the DI water in the recipe.

Thickness enters LCOW in two places, which is the trade the outer search resolves. A thicker
gel holds more water but desorbs more slowly and swells further, which acts through
$\bar m$. And $C_{sorb}$ is linear in $H_0$. The controls act only through $\bar m$.

\textbf{For fixed $x$, LCOW is strictly decreasing in $\bar m$.} This property justifies
the decomposition in Section~\ref{sec:decomp}.

\section{The full problem}
\label{sec:full}

For each cell $c$, choose a design and a control policy:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x \in \mathcal{X},\; \pi \in \Pi} \quad & \mathrm{LCOW}\bigl(x, \bar m(x, u_{1:D})\bigr) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & (m_d, s_{d+1}, \kappa_d) = F(x, u_d, s_d; w_{c,d}), \quad d = 1,\dots,D, \\
& s_1 \text{ cyclic (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cyclic})}, \\
& u_d = \pi_d(\mathcal{I}_d) \in \mathcal{U}, \\
& g(x) := \bar\kappa(x, u_{1:D}) - \kappa_{\max} \le 0, \qquad \kappa_{\max} = 0.05 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:full}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Swelling constraint.} A design is infeasible if its gel sits on the swelling
ceiling on more than 5\% of days (\code{optimize.CAPPED\_DAY\_FRACTION\_MAX}). On those
days, uptake is set by the clearance rather than by the isotherm; in the real device, this
is brine reaching the condenser. The constraint depends on the climate. A share-of-days
threshold is a blunt stand-in for a leakage model, which the repo does not have. A design
with no finite positive yield is also infeasible, and is assigned $g = 1$. Infeasible
designs score $\mathrm{LCOW}_{pen} = 10^4$~USD/m$^3$ (\code{optimize.lcow\_and\_constraint}).

\paragraph{Policy class $\Pi$.} The policy class has two independent axes. The \emph{tilt
mode} says how often $\theta$ may change:
\begin{align}
\text{three per day:} &\quad \theta^{(1)}_d, \theta^{(2)}_d, \theta^{(3)}_d \in \Theta_{15} \text{ free}, \\
\text{daily:}         &\quad \theta^{(1)}_d = \theta^{(2)}_d = \theta^{(3)}_d \in \Theta_5, \\
\text{seasonal:}      &\quad \theta^{(k)}_d = \theta_{q(d)} \in \Theta_5, \quad q(d) \in \{1,2,3,4\} \text{ the calendar quarter}.
\end{align}
The seal and open offsets are re-chosen every day in all three modes. Seasonal is a subset
of daily. Three-per-day and daily overlap but do not nest, because they use different tilt
grids.

The \emph{information regime} sets what the controller knows, $\mathcal{I}_d$, when it
picks $u_d$. It sees decision features $\tilde\phi_{c,d}$ in place of the true $\phi_{c,d}$
(\code{optimize.decision\_features}):
\begin{align}
\text{hindsight:}   &\quad \tilde\phi_{c,d} = \phi_{c,d} \quad \text{(perfect foresight; an upper bound)}, \\
\text{persistence:} &\quad \tilde\phi_{c,d} = \phi_{c,d-1} \quad \text{(day-ahead forecast stand-in; day 1 uses day } D), \\
\text{monthly:}     &\quad \tilde\phi_{c,d} = \tfrac{1}{|M(d)|}\textstyle\sum_{d' \in M(d)} \phi_{c,d'} \quad \text{(mean day of its month)}, \\
\text{yearly:}      &\quad \tilde\phi_{c,d} = \tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{d'=1}^{D} \phi_{c,d'} \quad \text{(mean day of the year)}.
\end{align}
The controller also knows its own gel state $\hat s_d$. A control is always \emph{chosen} on
$\tilde\phi$ and \emph{scored} on the real day $\phi_{c,d}$. The monthly and yearly means are
taken over the same 2024 year, a single-year stand-in for a multi-year climatology. The 3
tilt modes and 4 regimes give 12 scenarios (\code{optimize.SCENARIOS}).

\section{Decomposition: design per cell, control per day}
\label{sec:decomp}

Since LCOW decreases in $\bar m$ for fixed $x$, the inner problem can be posed in water
rather than dollars:
\begin{equation}
\min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \; \mathrm{LCOW}\bigl(x, \bar m^\ast(x)\bigr)
\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\; g\bigl(x, u^\ast_{1:D}(x)\bigr) \le 0,
\qquad
u^\ast_{1:D}(x) \in \argmax_{\pi \in \Pi} \bar m(x, u_{1:D}),
\label{eq:bilevel}
\end{equation}
with $\bar m^\ast(x) = \bar m(x, u^\ast_{1:D}(x))$. The swelling constraint couples the two
levels, but the inner level ignores it. Controls maximize water, and feasibility is checked
only at the design level. So Eq.~\eqref{eq:bilevel} is a restriction of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:full}: a design that would become feasible under a slightly lower-yield,
less-swelling schedule is rejected rather than rescued.

Keeping the inner level in physical units has a practical benefit. Every map can be
regenerated under new cost assumptions by re-applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:lcow}, with no
retraining.

\section{Surrogate day map}
\label{sec:surrogate}

The inner level evaluates up to $1.4\times10^5$ controls per day, per design, per cell,
which rules out calling $F$ directly. It uses a learned day map instead
(\code{surrogates}):
\begin{equation}
g_\psi(x, u, s, \phi) \;\to\; \bigl(\hat m,\; \hat s',\; \hat p_\kappa\bigr) \approx
\bigl(m_d,\; s_{d+1},\; \Pr[\kappa_d = 1]\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its 35 inputs are $H_0$ (mm), the 5 controls, the 2 state variables and the 27 day
features. The targets are $\log(1+m)$, the end-of-day state and the cap flag, and every
regression target is standardized on the training rows. Chained predictions of the state are
clipped to the range seen in training, and $\hat\kappa_d = \mathbb{1}[\hat p_\kappa > 0.5]$.

\subsection{Day features}
\label{sec:features}

$\phi_{c,d}$ joins two blocks (\code{climate}):
\begin{itemize}
\item the leading $k = 20$ principal components of the day's standardized hourly
$[T, \mathrm{RH}, \mathrm{GHI}]$ vector ($3\times24 = 72$ numbers; the PCA is fitted over
cached days worldwide);
\item seven scalars: RH at the diurnal $T$ minimum, the daytime GHI integral, the dewpoint
depression at peak sun, the diurnal $T$ amplitude, elevation, $|\text{latitude}|$, and noon
zenith.
\end{itemize}
The last two scalars carry the sun path, which tilt acts through. The value of $k$ comes from
the earlier \code{validate-features} study. That study ran the physics on real years and on
years rebuilt from $k$ components: at $k = 20$, the median annual-yield error was 0.5--3\%
in every climate regime.

Each cell is summarized by a 55-dimensional descriptor: the annual mean and standard
deviation of every day feature, plus the seasonal RH amplitude. Sampling, the train/test
split and out-of-distribution flags are all computed on standardized descriptors.

\subsection{Training locations}

The training locations are chosen in two stages (\code{climate.select\_cells}). Both use
greedy space-filling subset selection (algorithm 16.8), with the coverage metric
\begin{equation}
d_{\max}(X, S) = \max_{x \in X} \min_{s \in S} \lVert s - x \rVert_2 ,
\end{equation}
the largest distance from any cell to its nearest selected cell (eq.~16.4). Starting from one
random cell, each step adds the cell that most reduces $d_{\max}$
(\code{climate.greedy\_maximin\_subset}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Space-filling stage:} 750 cells, chosen from all $\approx$14.7k.
\item \textbf{Desert stage:} 250 more cells, by the same greedy restricted to the
K\"oppen--Geiger hot and cold deserts (BWh, BWk; Rubel et al.\ 2017, 2,417 cells). It is
seeded with the deserts the first stage already took. The extra cells therefore fill gaps in
desert climate space rather than piling onto its densest part. Deserts are oversampled because
they are rare by area but decisive for the headline result.
\end{enumerate}
The minimax criterion spends samples evenly across climate space, whatever the density of
real cells. That differs from density-weighted schemes such as $k$-means.

The 1,000 cells are split 70/15/15 into training, validation and test sets, stratified by
K\"oppen group. The split is by \emph{cell}, never by row. A cell's rows are thousands of
correlated days of one climate, and a row split would leak each climate across the split.

\subsection{Training data}

Each cell gets 5 thicknesses by stratified sampling (section 16.4): one uniform draw inside
each fifth of $\mathcal{X}$ (\code{simulate.stratified\_thicknesses}). Each (cell, thickness)
pair is simulated as one chained physics year, with $u_d$ drawn \emph{independently every
day} (\code{simulate.random\_controls}):
\begin{itemize}
\item the offsets are drawn uniformly from $\mathcal{S}$;
\item on half the days, a single tilt from $\Theta_5$ is used for all three segments;
\item on the other half, three independent tilts are drawn from $\Theta_5$.
\end{itemize}
The equal-tilt half is deliberate. The daily and seasonal scenarios only ever query equal
tilts, a measure-zero set under fully independent draws.

One year therefore yields $\sim$365 rows that cover the control space, and the states in
those rows are ones real operation produces. Each year's first day is dropped, because its
start state comes from the Aitken extrapolation rather than from a simulated previous day.
The campaign totals 5,000 physics years, about $1.8\times10^6$ rows.

\subsection{Model families and selection}

Three families are each fitted over a hyperparameter grid (\code{surrogates.GRIDS}). Every fit
sees the training cells only.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.62\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Family} & \textbf{Grid, and how it iterates} \\
\midrule
Ridge regression & $\alpha \in \{10^{-4}, \dots, 10^4\}$ (9 values); closed form \\
Random forest & max features $\in \{0.33, 0.66, 1\}$ $\times$ min leaf $\in \{1, 5, 20\}$;
grown 25 trees at a time to 300, each tree bagging 10\% of the rows \\
MLP & width $\in \{64, 128, 256, 512\}$ $\times$ depth $\in \{2, 3, 4\}$ $\times$
learning rate $\in \{10^{-3}, 3\times10^{-3}\}$; SiLU activations; Adam with cosine decay;
20,000 steps of batch 4,096; validation-best checkpoint, checked every 500 steps \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The MLP fits the cap flag with a logistic loss, weighted equally with the three regression
MSEs. The other families regress the flag as a score in $[0,1]$. Each configuration's
convergence is recorded: MLP training and validation loss per step, and forest size.

Within each family, the configuration with the lowest daily water RMSE on the validation cells
is selected. The test cells are scored once, for that configuration only. Two kinds of test
error are reported:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{daily error:} one-day water given the true start state;
\item \textbf{annual error:} the surrogate chained through each test year under the physics'
own controls, from the physics' first kept state. This is the number that says whether state
error compounds, and the one LCOW depends on.
\end{itemize}
Both are broken out by K\"oppen group (\code{surrogates.evaluate}).

The inner loop needs on the order of $10^{12}$ predictions per scenario, so the deployed model
must run on the GPU. The deployed surrogate is therefore the better of ridge and the MLP on the
validation criterion. A fitted ridge model is exactly a depth-0 MLP, so both families share
one compiled JAX predictor (\code{surrogates.deployable}). The random forest is reported for
comparison but cannot be deployed.

\section{Solution method}

\subsection{Inner level: enumeration on the surrogate}

There is no gradient step and no local-minimum risk, and the result is a discrete schedule,
which is what a real controller runs (\code{optimize.make\_year\_fn}). Let $\mathcal{U}_d$
denote the day's candidate set. The \textbf{greedy daily walk} is
\begin{align}
u_d &= \argmax_{u \in \mathcal{U}_d} \hat m\bigl(x, u, \hat s_d, \tilde\phi_{c,d}\bigr), \\
(\hat m_d, \hat s_{d+1}) &= g_\psi\bigl(x, u_d, \hat s_d, \phi_{c,d}\bigr),
\end{align}
where the candidate set depends on the tilt mode:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{three per day:} $\mathcal{S}^2 \times \Theta_{15}^3$, 136,125 candidates;
\item \textbf{daily:} $\mathcal{S}^2 \times \Theta_5$ with equal tilts, 14,157 candidates;
\item \textbf{seasonal:} $\mathcal{S}^2$ at the quarter's tilt, 1,089 candidates. At the
start of each quarter, the walk is planned through the whole quarter under each of the 13
levels, using the decision features only. The level with the largest planned water is kept,
and the quarter is then lived under it, with its end state chained into the next quarter.
The tilt is thus chosen with the same information as the daily controls, never with the
real weather.
\end{itemize}
The cyclic start (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cyclic}) is approximated by 8 rounds of the day-1 greedy step
from the physics' initial loading.

The greedy walk is \textbf{myopic}: it ignores what today's choice does to tomorrow's starting
state. The exact version of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bilevel} is the dynamic program
\begin{equation}
V_d(s) = \max_{u \in \mathcal{U}_d} \Bigl[\hat m(x, u, s, \tilde\phi_{c,d})
+ V_{d+1}\bigl(\hat s'(x, u, s, \tilde\phi_{c,d})\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
solved backwards over a discretized state. Greedy is exact when overnight absorption
saturates, because the start state then does not depend on yesterday's choice. Its cost
elsewhere is not measured (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).

\subsection{Outer level: constrained Bayesian optimization over $\mathcal{X}$}

For each cell, the outer level minimizes
$J_c(x) = \mathrm{LCOW}(x, \hat{\bar m}^\ast(x))$ subject to $g(x) \le 0$, over the
finite candidate set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}_h = \{1.00, 1.05, \dots, 10.00\}\,\si{\milli\metre} \quad (181 \text{ thicknesses}),
\end{equation}
which is fabrication resolution. BO, its tuning ground truth and the global run all evaluate
this same set (\code{optimize.run\_bo}, \code{optimize.propose}). The setup:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective model:} a GP regressor fitted on the unit interval to the feasible
points only, with a normalized target. The kernel is chosen by tuning (Section~\ref{sec:tuning}).
\item \textbf{Constraint model} (Gardner et al.\ 2014): a second GP, Mat\'ern-5/2 plus white
noise, fitted to every evaluated constraint value $g$. Its predictive distribution gives
\begin{equation}
P(\text{feasible} \mid x) = \Phi\!\left(-\mu_g(x)/\sigma_g(x)\right).
\end{equation}
Modelling the continuous $g$ is used rather than classifying the binary label because a
Laplace GP classifier's probabilities stay soft (about 0.2--0.3) even inside a region ringed
by infeasible samples. EI's large extrapolated variance there then outweighs them; this was
observed on a synthetic test.
\item \textbf{Acquisition} (chapter 19), maximized exactly over $\mathcal{X}_h$, with
$z = (y^\ast - \mu - \xi)/\sigma$ and $y^\ast$ the best feasible value so far:
\begin{align}
\text{EI:} &\quad \bigl[(y^\ast - \mu - \xi)\,\Phi(z) + \sigma\,\varphi(z)\bigr]\, P(\text{feasible}), \\
\text{PI:} &\quad \Phi(z)\, P(\text{feasible}), \\
\text{error-based:} &\quad \sigma\, P(\text{feasible}), \\
\text{LCB:} &\quad -(\mu - \kappa\sigma) \text{ over } \{P(\text{feasible}) \ge 0.5\}, \\
\text{prediction-based:} &\quad -\mu \text{ over } \{P(\text{feasible}) \ge 0.5\}.
\end{align}
Signed scores cannot be weighted by a probability, so LCB and prediction-based search are
restricted to the region the constraint model calls feasible.
\item \textbf{Initial design:} a Latin hypercube (section 16.3) snapped to the candidates.
Until two feasible points exist there is nothing to regress, so the next point is the one
most likely to be feasible (Gelbart et al.\ 2014), with ties broken by distance from
everything tried.
\item \textbf{Batches:} where a round proposes several points, Kriging-Believer (Ginsbourger
et al.\ 2010).
\end{itemize}
Cells advance in lockstep, so each round costs one batched surrogate sweep across all cells.

\subsection{Tuning the optimizer}
\label{sec:tuning}

The kernel, the acquisition and the GP settings are chosen against known optima (the
\code{tune-grid} and \code{tune-bo} stages of \code{train}). On 60 validation cells,
stratified by K\"oppen group, the surrogate-optimal year is evaluated at every
$x \in \mathcal{X}_h$ under daily tilt and hindsight. The feasible minimum of that grid is
each cell's true optimum $J_c^\ast$ on the surrogate. Each BO configuration is then replayed
against those values and scored by the mean relative simple regret
\begin{equation}
r_c(n) = \frac{\min_{k \le n,\; g_k \le 0} J_c(x_k)}{J_c^\ast} - 1 .
\end{equation}
Two sweeps are run:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every kernel (RBF; Mat\'ern-1/2, -3/2 and -5/2; rational quadratic; Mat\'ern-5/2 plus
linear) $\times$ every acquisition (EI with $\xi \in \{0, 0.01, 0.1\}$; PI with
$\xi \in \{0.01, 0.1\}$; LCB with $\kappa \in \{1, 2, 3\}$; prediction-based; error-based),
at 3 initial points and a budget of 15;
\item for the winner, initial points $\in \{2,3,4,6\}$ $\times$ GP restarts
$\in \{0,2,8\}$ $\times$ white-noise term on or off.
\end{enumerate}
Configurations are ranked by mean regret at the end of the budget, with ties broken by the
area under the regret curve. The run budget is the first evaluation count at which the
chosen configuration's worst-cell regret falls to 0.1\%, capped at 15. Validation cells are used so
that tuning never touches the test cells the validation in Section~\ref{sec:validation}
scores.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Validation (\code{validate}) compares BO with and without the surrogate, on the same problem,
at 40 test cells stratified by K\"oppen group. Test cells are untouched by training, early
stopping, model selection and BO tuning.

\paragraph{The shared problem.} True physics cannot afford daily control: that would be
14k candidates $\times$ 366 days per evaluation. The shared problem is therefore the one it
can afford, thickness plus one annual schedule:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\min_{x' \in \mathcal{X}'} \mathrm{LCOW}\bigl(x', \bar m(x')\bigr) \;\;\text{s.t.}\;\; g(x') \le 0, \\
x' = (H_0, \delta_s, \delta_o, \theta), \qquad u_d = (\delta_s, \delta_o, \theta, \theta, \theta)\;\; \forall d .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Its candidate set is a 16,384-point scrambled Sobol cloud. In it, $H_0$ is snapped to the
\SI{0.05}{\milli\metre} grid, the offsets to the \SI{0.25}{\hour} grid and $\theta$ to
\SI{1}{\degree}.

\paragraph{The two searches.} Both run the tuned kernel and acquisition with automatic
relevance determination over the 4 dimensions, 8 initial points, batches of 8 and a budget of
40.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Surrogate BO:} evaluates $\bar m$ by chaining $g_\psi$ through the year under the
schedule; its optimum is then re-scored with physics.
\item \textbf{Physics BO:} evaluates $\bar m$ with true physics directly.
\end{itemize}
The headline number per cell is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_c = \mathrm{LCOW}_F\bigl(x_c'^{\,\text{surr}}\bigr) - \mathrm{LCOW}_F\bigl(x_c'^{\,\text{phys}}\bigr)
\quad [\text{USD/m}^3],
\end{equation}
which is negative when the surrogate-assisted search found the better optimum.

The report also gives the surrogate's prediction error at its own optimum. A positive bias
there is the optimizer's winner's curse: it picks what the surrogate overrates. Finally,
every scenario's design and daily schedule from the global run is re-scored with physics at
the same cells. Compared with the physics-BO annual optimum, this shows the value of daily
control under true physics, net of the greedy walk's myopia; the two cannot be separated.
